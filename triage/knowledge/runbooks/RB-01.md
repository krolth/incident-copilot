id: RB-01
title: Revert a bad deploy or configuration change
category: bad_change
---
## When to use
Errors or latency began within minutes of a code deploy or a configuration or feature-flag push, and the onset time lines up with the change.

## Signals
- The fault starts after a deploy of the affected service, or after a configuration push that is not listed as a deploy. Check the logs of config and flag tooling as well as the deploy list.
- Only instances or hosts running the new version are affected (compare per-host error rates and versions).
- A dependency looks unhealthy only because the changed service is sending it more traffic, for example after retry or pool-size changes. Compare the dependency's request rate against its own health.
- Memory, connections, or retries grow only on the new version. A restart will only delay the problem; revert the version instead.

## Steps
1. Identify the exact change and its timestamp from deploy history and configuration tooling.
2. Revert the change (roll back the deploy or restore the previous configuration value) on affected instances first.
3. Watch error rate and latency for ten minutes; confirm recovery matches the revert time.
4. Open a ticket for the change owner with the evidence. Do not re-apply the change until fixed.

## Do not
Restart or scale out first; this hides the cause and prolongs the incident.
