id: RB-04
title: Respond to a traffic spike beyond capacity
category: traffic_spike
---
## When to use
Real demand (the weekly arena, a broadcast, a streamer going live) pushes the platform past its capacity, and
nothing is broken in the logs apart from the consequences of overload. Mitigate by adding capacity and shedding
low-value work.

## Signals
- gateway `request_rate_rps`, logins, and queue joins rise several times above the usual curve for that hour.
- Known demand: Friday 19:00 UTC arena; a broadcast; a streamer going live (logins and queue joins up within
  minutes, spectators piling onto one game).
- Autoscaling at its maximum (gateway 40 pods, matchmaking 16) or game-server capacity near 40,000 games; gateway
  503 "no game-server capacity"; 429s rising as rate limits protect the system.
- A single hot game-server host with normal `active_games` but very high connections, CPU and
  `move_latency_p99_ms`: the allocator balances by game count, so a host carrying a popular game's spectators runs
  hot. That is load, not a failed node.

## Steps
1. Confirm the demand source (calendar, streamer or broadcast, traffic by client) so you do not chase a fault.
2. Raise the autoscaling maximum of the saturated service; for game servers, boot extra hosts (about 6 minutes
   each, quota 14) and check they register as `accepting`.
3. For a hot game-server host: cordon it so it gets no new games; cap spectators per game (config key
   `spectate.max_per_game`) to protect the players on that host.
4. Shed low-priority work: pause `anticheat-backfill` and other scheduled jobs; lower leaderboard and stats refresh
   rates.
5. Watch latency settle; afterwards, plan a capacity review and allocation by load rather than game count.

## Do not
- Roll back deploys from earlier in the day without evidence they are related.
- Restart or drain the hot game-server host: its players' games would be aborted or migrated during peak.
