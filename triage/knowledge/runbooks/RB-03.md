---
id: RB-03
title: Contain a resource leak
category: resource_leak
---
## When to use
A resource (memory, file descriptors, Redis memory, disk, connections) grows steadily over hours or days towards its
limit, it does not fall when traffic falls, and no recent change lines up with the start of the growth.
If the growth started right after a deploy or config push, see RB-01 instead.

## Signals
- A slow, steady trend that a one-hour view hides: look at 24 hours and 7 days.
- Game servers: `open_fds` keeps rising while `active_games` follows its normal daily cycle (sockets not closed after
  the heartbeat timeout); "too many open files" near the 65,536 limit; memory growing with it.
- redis-live: `used_memory_pct` grows day after day; keys that should expire (game checkpoints have a 2 h TTL) have
  no TTL; near the limit, evictions start and every client slows down. The alert may fire on a client (game-server
  move latency) rather than on Redis.
- Out-of-memory kills or "no space left on device" at the end of the trend.

## Steps
1. Confirm the trend over a long window and estimate time to the limit; that decides how urgent this is.
2. Capture diagnostics before anything else: open descriptor list (`ls /proc/<pid>/fd | wc -l`, socket states),
   heap profile, or for Redis a key sample with `MEMORY USAGE` and `TTL`.
3. Reclaim the resource without hurting players:
   - game servers: one host at a time, cordon it, let games finish (or migrate them), restart it, uncordon, and check
     the trend resets before the next host;
   - stateless pods: restart one pod at a time;
   - redis-live: set the missing TTLs or delete the keys of finished games with a script; do not flush (it holds live
     game checkpoints and queues).
4. File a defect with the diagnostics and growth rate, and keep watching until the fix ships.

## Do not
- Restart every host at once, or restart before capturing diagnostics.
- Restart game-server hosts without draining them: it aborts the games on them.
